Title: Parity-independent optimal eavesdropping on advantage distillation in quantum key distribution

Abstract (1415 characters):
In advantage distillation for quantum key distribution the legitimate parties announce the parities of blocks of raw bits and keep the blocks on which they agree. An eavesdropper who waits for the announcement discriminates two known product states with the Helstrom measurement, which depends on the announced parity. We prove that she does not need to wait. For every block size we construct one projective measurement, the orbit of a single Helstrom pair under Pauli operators, that attains the Helstrom success probability in all parity classes simultaneously; the parity is needed only to read the outcome. Hiding the parity announcement behind a delay or a cipher therefore leaves her guessing probability unchanged, and with it the 20% error-rate limit of repetition-code advantage distillation for BB84; in the min-entropy accounting the finite-key threshold of decoy-state BB84 is the collective-attack value, 17.66% at 10^14 pulses. The 25% entanglement limit known for an eavesdropper who measures each probe separately requires more than a hidden parity: she must be unable to bring the probes of a block together, which a block partition drawn at random after the quantum phase enforces on a short-lived memory. Under a stated per-probe model the threshold is then 20.7–20.8% at nine-bit blocks, whereas semidefinite programmes show that a memory holding six probes recovers most of the adaptive value.
