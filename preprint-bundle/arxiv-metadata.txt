Title: Does hiding the post-processing from a memory-limited eavesdropper raise the noise tolerance of QKD? Timelocked advantage distillation and the 20% barrier

Abstract (1913 characters):
Advantage distillation (AD) raises the finite-key threshold QBER of decoy-state BB84 from 9.5% to 17.3% (Treplin, Kleinpaß and Orsucci, arXiv:2511.21665); asymptotically two-way post-processing tolerates 20%, and Bae and Acín showed that an adversary who stores her probes until the AD parities are announced attains this limit, while one forced to measure each probe beforehand is held off until the 25% entanglement limit. We ask what a quantum computational timelock on the post-processing announcements can buy for this protocol, treating the parities, the block partition and the bases separately. Hiding the parities buys nothing: we prove that for every block size the best measurement made before the parities are known attains the adaptive Helstrom value, so the key length in the min-entropy accounting is the collective-attack value, 17.66% at $10^{14}$ pulses against the 17.30% certified by the entropic-uncertainty bound of that work. Hiding the partition is different: their protocol partitions the sifted key at random after the quantum phase, so an adversary whose memory is shorter than the time between the probes of a block cannot bring them together. Modelling her by role-blind per-probe measurements, an assumption we state rather than derive, the Helstrom benchmark, which a numerical search over identical measurements does not improve at odd block sizes, gives 20.8% (Shannon) and 20.7% (min-entropy) at $b=9$, 19.4% and 20.6% in a post-selection sensitivity check, with 25% as asymptote. Whether a memory holding many probes without the partition can do better is open; at up to six probes a joint measurement recovers most of the adaptive value, so at these sizes the gain rests on locality. Hiding the basis leaves the BB84 guessing probability unchanged at one- and two-bit blocks and, for six-state, shrinks the memoryless gain by an order of magnitude at two- and three-bit blocks.
