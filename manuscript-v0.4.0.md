# Does hiding the post-processing from a memory-limited eavesdropper raise the noise tolerance of QKD? Timelocked advantage distillation and the 20% barrier

## Abstract

The noise tolerance of quantum key distribution (QKD) is set by its classical post-processing: for decoy-state BB84, advantage distillation (AD) raises the finite-key threshold quantum bit error rate (QBER) from about 9.5% to about 17.3% [T25]. It is tempting to go further by exploiting a weaker adversary: if the parities and syndromes that AD and reconciliation disclose are kept from the eavesdropper for longer than her quantum memory survives (a quantum computational timelock, realised by a plain delay or by a post-quantum cipher), she must measure before she knows them, and her side information becomes classical. We analyse this proposal in the finite-key regime, reusing the decoy-state statistics of Ref. [T25] and replacing only the adversary term. The answer depends on what the adversary may do with her probes before the timelock expires. If she measures them one at a time, the incoherent-attack analysis of Gisin and Wolf applies, the finite-key threshold at $10^{14}$ pulses rises from 17.3% to 20.7–21.9%, and the asymptotic threshold approaches the 25% entanglement limit. If she may measure the probes of an AD block jointly, even without knowing the parities, a semidefinite programme shows that she already attains the adaptive Helstrom value, her error on the distilled bit decays as $c_\psi^{2b}$ rather than $|c_\psi|^{b}$, the asymptotic threshold falls to 20% (the known unconditional two-way limit of BB84), and the finite-key threshold at $10^{14}$ pulses returns to 17.3–18.4% depending on the entropy accounting, against 17.3% for the entropic-uncertainty result. Hiding the post-processing therefore buys nothing beyond what unconditional security already provides; the apparent gain is an artefact of the per-probe measurement assumption, and the 25% limit of Gisin and Wolf is itself a statement about per-probe measurements. Hiding the basis as well does not rescue the idea: for BB84 the worst-case attack is unaffected, and for the six-state protocol, where a memoryless adversary is known to lose five percentage points without advantage distillation, a joint measurement of a two-bit block with independent hidden bases recovers the adaptive value to within $10^{-3}$. The joint-measurement model reproduces the unconditional two-way limits of 20% (BB84) and 27.6% (six-state) in closed form, which is why the timelock cannot beat them. A by-product of independent use is that the key-length formula of Ref. [T25] must be applied only inside its stated domain $\phi_X+\phi_Z<1/2$, outside of which an unconstrained optimiser certifies key at impossible QBERs.

## 1. Introduction

Quantum key distribution promises keys whose secrecy does not rest on computational assumptions, but the promise is only redeemed when the channel is quiet enough. Every QKD protocol has a maximum QBER above which no key can be certified, and for the most deployed protocol, decoy-state BB84 with one-way post-processing, that maximum is close to 9–11% depending on the source model [T25, Lim14]. Daylight free-space links, fibres shared with bright classical traffic, and long satellite passes routinely approach or exceed this level, so raising the threshold without changing the optics is one of the few remaining software levers on QKD range.

Advantage distillation is the classical lever. Introduced by Maurer for classical key agreement [Maurer93] and brought into QKD by Gottesman and Lo [GL03], it post-selects blocks of raw bits whose announced parities agree, so that the surviving bits are far better correlated between Alice and Bob than between Alice and Eve. Chau showed that BB84 tolerates 20.0% and six-state QKD 27.6% asymptotically with iterated two-way post-processing [Chau02], a finite-key treatment for sending-or-not-sending twin-field QKD was given by Jiang et al. [Jiang21], and for decoy-state BB84 the first complete finite-key analysis is that of Treplin, Kleinpaß and Orsucci [T25], who proved that a $b$-bit repetition-code AD step raises the finite-key threshold from 9.5% to 17.3% at $N=10^{14}$ pulses. Their formula bounds Eve's smooth min-entropy about the distilled bits through an entropic uncertainty relation on the post-selected state, and it allows the adversary to hold a purification of everything until the last message of the protocol has been announced.

A different line of work asks what changes if the adversary cannot wait. Watanabe, Matsumoto and Uyematsu encrypted the parities of a two-way pre-processing step with a pre-shared one-time pad and obtained a higher net key rate in the Shor–Preskill framework [WMU06]. Pastushenko and Kronberg encrypted the error-correction data and argued that, if Eve's quantum memory decoheres before she learns the codeword set, her information is bounded by the additive accessible information rather than by the Holevo quantity, so collective attacks collapse to individual ones [PK23]; they did not claim composable security. Novak then proved, in the non-asymptotic framework of Tomamichel and Leverrier [TL17], that one-time-pad protection of one-way error-correction data drawn from the same session leaves the key rate exactly unchanged at fixed security level [Novak25], and Fung, Ma, Chau and Cai had shown that delaying privacy amplification leaves security and key rate unchanged [FMCC12]. Neither encryption nor delay by itself buys anything; whatever gain exists must come from an assumption about the adversary's memory. Vyas and Alléaume made that assumption explicit as the quantum computational hybrid model, in which a computationally secure cipher cannot be broken before a time $t_{\rm comp}$ and any quantum memory decoheres within $t_{\rm coh}<t_{\rm comp}$; their MUB-QCT protocol timelocks the basis information of a high-dimensional encoding and achieves everlasting security at rates beyond QKD [VA20, MBBA25].

This paper asks whether the same assumption helps the most deployed protocol when the hardware is left untouched and only the AD and reconciliation messages are timelocked. Under the timelock the adversary must measure before she learns the parities, so her side information is classical and non-adaptive, and the problem becomes one of state discrimination with post-measurement information [BWW08]. Our contributions are: (i) a two-tier threat model that states precisely which announcement delays make the adversary classical, and the observation that a post-quantum cipher serves only to remove the latency of a plain delay; (ii) a finite-key secure key length that reuses the decoy-state statistics of Ref. [T25] and replaces only the adversary term, implemented numerically and validated by reproducing all four published thresholds of that work; (iii) the result that if the adversary measures each probe individually, the threshold at $N=10^{14}$ rises to 20.7–21.9% with an asymptote at the 25% entanglement limit of Gisin and Wolf [GW99]; (iv) the result that if she may measure the probes of a block jointly, a semidefinite programme shows that the best non-adaptive joint measurement already equals the adaptive one, the asymptote falls to 20%, and the finite-key threshold returns to that of Ref. [T25], so that hiding the post-processing does not beat unconditional security; and (v) the clarification that the classical 25% limit of Ref. [GW99] presupposes per-probe measurements. We also record a condition on the domain of validity of the key-length formula of Ref. [T25] that any implementation must enforce.

## 2. Model and assumptions

**A1 (quantum computational timelock).** A cipher $E_k$ shared by Alice and Bob cannot be broken by the adversary before time $t_{\rm comp}$ after its use; any quantum memory available to the adversary decoheres within $t_{\rm coh}<t_{\rm comp}$ [VA20]. The technology assessment in Ref. [VA20] places $t_{\rm coh}$ of optical quantum memories below one second, whereas $t_{\rm comp}$ for a post-quantum cipher is measured in years; the assumption is only required to hold during the protocol run, which is what makes the resulting key everlasting.

**A2 (announcement delay).** Let $t_{\rm rx}$ be the time at which the adversary intercepts a pulse and $t_m$ the time at which a post-processing message $m$ is sent. Message $m$ is hidden from the adversary for a delay $\Delta_m$, realised either as a plain delay $\tau_{\rm ann}$ before publication or by encryption under $E_k$ (then $\Delta_m=t_{\rm comp}-t_m$). The requirement is
$$\Delta_m > t_{\rm coh} - (t_m - t_{\rm rx}).$$
In tier V-A only the AD parities $c_A, c_B$ and the reconciliation syndrome are delayed and the basis announcement is immediate; in tier V-B the basis announcement is delayed as well. For a protocol that keys only from $Z$-basis events, as Protocol 1 of Ref. [T25] does, V-B is no stronger than V-A: the adversary optimises her measurement for the $Z$ events she cares about, and the hidden basis does not change her task. We therefore analyse V-A.

**A3 (physical layer).** As in Ref. [T25]: phase-randomised coherent states with three intensities $\mu_1>\mu_2+\mu_3$, $\mu_3\approx 0$, active basis choice, threshold detectors with random assignment of double clicks, and an authenticated classical channel.

**A4 (adversary).** Each pulse interacts with an independent ancilla through the symmetric unitary of Refs. [FGGNP97, GW99] (a collective attack). Before $t_{\rm coh}$ the adversary may apply any measurement to her ancillas; after it, her side information is classical. By A2 the measurement does not depend on the delayed messages. We consider two classes: **individual**, in which each ancilla is measured separately after the basis announcement; and **joint**, in which the $b$ ancillas of an AD block are measured together, still before the parities are known. The reduction from coherent to collective attacks through the post-selection technique and the question of joint measurements across blocks are discussed in Section 7.

**A5 (statistics).** Finite-size fluctuations of the decoy estimates, the block post-selection and the error-rate extrapolation are handled exactly as in Ref. [T25]; we also verify the headline numbers with the virtual-i.i.d. reduction of Ref. [Jiang21].

The role of the cipher follows from A2. A plain delay $\tau_{\rm ann}>t_{\rm coh}$ is sufficient and needs no cryptography, at the price of a key-delivery latency of $\tau_{\rm ann}$. Encryption under a post-quantum cipher removes that latency while keeping the adversary's delay at $t_{\rm comp}$. The cipher compresses latency; it does not create the gain, which is the reading of Ref. [PK23] that survives the equivalence theorem of Ref. [Novak25].

## 3. The adversary's information on a distilled bit

Under A1 and A2 the adversary's optimal strategy is an immediate measurement followed by classical decoding once the delayed messages become available (Ref. [VA20], Proposition 1). In the symmetric attack each pulse interacts as
$$U|\!\uparrow\rangle|0\rangle=\sqrt{F}|\!\uparrow\rangle\psi_\uparrow+\sqrt{D}|\!\downarrow\rangle\varphi_\uparrow,\qquad U|\!\downarrow\rangle|0\rangle=\sqrt{F}|\!\downarrow\rangle\psi_\downarrow+\sqrt{D}|\!\uparrow\rangle\varphi_\downarrow,$$
with disturbance $D$ equal to the single-photon QBER and $F=(1+c_\varphi)/(2-c_\psi+c_\varphi)$, $c_\psi=\langle\psi_\uparrow|\psi_\downarrow\rangle$, $c_\varphi=\langle\varphi_\uparrow|\varphi_\downarrow\rangle$ [GW99]. The $\psi$ and $\varphi$ subspaces are orthogonal, so the adversary can always learn whether a bit was disturbed ($Z_1=X\oplus Y$) without affecting anything else. After a $b$-bit block is accepted and both parity strings are announced, she knows whether the block was accepted with all bits correct (probability $p_u=(1-D)^b/p_a$, $p_a=(1-D)^b+D^b$) or all bits wrong, and in the first case her $b$ ancillas are in the product state $\psi_{a_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\psi_{a_b}$, where the parities fix all $a_i$ relative to the distilled bit $a_1$.

**Individual measurement.** Measuring each ancilla by the Helstrom measurement gives a guess of each bit that is correct with probability $\delta_0=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\sqrt{1-c_\psi^2}$ on undisturbed bits and $\delta_1=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\sqrt{1-c_\varphi^2}$ on disturbed ones; with the parities the guesses become $b$ votes on $a_1$ and a majority vote has error $\gamma_b(\delta)$. The adversary's information on the distilled bit is
$$I_E^{\rm ind}(D,b)=\max_{c_\varphi}\Big[p_u\,(1-h(\gamma_b(\delta_0)))+(1-p_u)\,(1-h(\gamma_b(\delta_1)))\Big],$$
and for every $b\ge2$ the maximum is at $c_\varphi=1$, $\delta_0=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\sqrt{1-(3-2/(1-D))^2}$. This is the incoherent-attack model of Ref. [GW99]; its Theorem 1 states that key agreement by AD is possible iff $D/(1-D)<2\sqrt{\delta_0(1-\delta_0)}=|c_\psi|$, which for the 4-state protocol is $D<1/4$.

**Joint measurement.** If the adversary may measure the $b$ ancillas together, but still before the parities are announced, her task is to discriminate the $2^b$ product states $\psi_{a_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\psi_{a_b}$ with the post-measurement information $c$, which partitions them into $2^{b-1}$ pairs $\{\psi_a,\psi_{\bar a}\}$ [BWW08]. Her success probability is the value of the semidefinite programme
$$P^{\rm joint}_b=\max_{\{M_f\}}\ 2^{-b}\sum_{a}\mathrm{Tr}\Big[\rho_a\sum_{f:\,f(c(a))=a_1}M_f\Big],\qquad M_f\ge0,\ \sum_f M_f=\mathbb 1,$$
over POVMs indexed by the functions $f:\{0,1\}^{b-1}\to\{0,1\}$ that assign a guess to every possible parity string. It is upper-bounded by the adaptive value $P^{\rm ad}_b=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\sqrt{1-c_\psi^{2b}}$, the Helstrom probability for the two product states of a known pair, since the single-copy overlap $c_\psi$ becomes $c_\psi^{b}$ for $b$ copies. Solving the programme for $b=2,3$ and $D\in[0.05,0.24]$ we find $P^{\rm joint}_b=P^{\rm ad}_b$ to within $10^{-8}$ (Table 2): the optimal measurements of the different pairs are compatible, so hiding the parities costs the adversary nothing. The adversary's information is then $I_E^{\rm joint}(D,b)=\max_{c_\varphi}[p_u(1-h(1-P^{\rm ad}_b(c_\psi)))+(1-p_u)(1-h(1-P^{\rm ad}_b(c_\varphi)))]$, which is also a valid bound for any measurement within the block since $P^{\rm ad}_b$ is an upper bound.

The consequence for the asymptotic threshold is immediate. Bob's error on an accepted block is $D^b/p_a\approx(D/(1-D))^b$. The adversary's error is $\approx|c_\psi|^b$ for individual measurement and $\approx c_\psi^{2b}/4$ for joint measurement. Key agreement by the repetition code requires the adversary's error to decay more slowly than Bob's, i.e. $|c_\psi|>D/(1-D)$ in the first case and $c_\psi^2>D/(1-D)$ in the second. With $c_\psi=(1-3D)/(1-D)$ at $c_\varphi=1$ these conditions read $D<1/4$ and $10D^2-7D+1>0$, i.e. $D<1/5$. The 25% limit of Ref. [GW99] is thus a statement about per-ancilla measurements; a joint measurement of the ancillas of one block, with no quantum memory beyond the measurement itself, lowers the limit to 20%, which is the unconditional two-way limit of BB84 established by Chau [Chau02].

The same algebra applied to the six-state protocol, whose symmetric attack has $c_\varphi=0$ and hence $c_\psi=(1-2D)/(1-D)$, gives $5D^2-5D+1>0$, i.e. $D<(5-\sqrt5)/10=27.6\%$, the limit of Chau [Chau02]. The joint-measurement model thus reproduces both unconditional two-way limits in closed form.

**Hiding the basis as well.** A protocol that keys from both bases and delays the sifting announcement beyond $t_{\rm coh}$ confronts the adversary with the basis as additional post-measurement information. Without advantage distillation ($b=1$) this is the memoryless-adversary setting solved by Bocquet, Alléaume and Leverrier [BAL11], who optimised Eve's interaction and POVM and found thresholds of 15.4% for BB84 (against 14.6% with memory), 17.6% for SARG04 and 20.4% for six-state (against 15.6%); the gain is confined to protocols with more than two bases, in line with the result of Ref. [BWW08] that two bases admit a common optimal measurement. We extended the semidefinite programme to hidden bases and parities, with Eve's probe states conditioned on Bob's outcome and the accepted-block mixture of all-correct and all-flipped blocks (`hidden_basis_sdp.py`). For BB84 the worst-case attack ($c_\varphi=1$) gives $P^{\rm joint}=P^{\rm ad}$ at $b=1$ and $b=2$ for every $D$; the gap that exists at $c_\varphi=0$ is irrelevant because the adversary chooses $c_\varphi$. For six-state at $b=1$ we reproduce the qualitative gain of Ref. [BAL11] (e.g. $P^{\rm joint}=0.750$ against $P^{\rm ad}=0.833$ at $D=0.167$). But as soon as blocks of two bits are formed the gain disappears: for blocks whose two pulses share an unknown basis, $P^{\rm joint}=P^{\rm ad}$ to $10^{-6}$ (0.888644 against 0.888642 at $D=0.167$, verified with two solvers), and for the realistic blocks with independently chosen hidden bases the upper bound in which only the bases are hidden gives $P^{\rm joint}\le 0.8878$ against $P^{\rm ad}=0.8886$ at $D=0.167$ and $0.9556$ against $0.9583$ at $D=0.259$, a difference worth less than $10^{-3}$ bits per raw bit. Hiding the basis and hiding the parities therefore do not compound: the one-way memoryless gain of Ref. [BAL11] (20.4% for six-state) is dominated by advantage distillation without any assumption (27.6%), and adding the timelock to advantage distillation changes nothing.

## 4. Finite-key secure key length

The protocol is Protocol 1 of Ref. [T25] with two modifications: the parity strings $c_A,c_B$ and the reconciliation syndrome are sent under $E_k$ (or after $\tau_{\rm ann}$), and privacy amplification uses the bound below. Let $S^-_{\bar K}$ be the lower bound on the number of accepted blocks consisting only of single-photon events, $\phi_Z^+$ the upper bound on the single-photon $Z$-basis error rate, $N_{\bar K}$ and $\Phi_{\bar K}$ the expected number and error rate of the distilled bits, and $\lambda_{\rm IR}=f N_{\bar K} h(\Phi_{\bar K})$ the reconciliation leakage, all as defined in Eqs. (12)–(20) of Ref. [T25]. Then
$$\ell_{\rm QCT}\le S^-_{\bar K}\big[1-I_E(\phi_Z^+,b)\big]-\Delta_{\rm AEP}(S^-_{\bar K},\epsilon)-\lambda_{\rm IR}-\log_2\frac{2}{\epsilon_{\rm cor}}-4\log_2\frac{2^{7/4}}{\epsilon_{\rm sec}-\epsilon_{\rm sec}^2/8},$$
with $I_E$ either $I_E^{\rm ind}$ or $I_E^{\rm joint}$, where $\Delta_{\rm AEP}=4\log_2(2+\sqrt2)\sqrt{S\log_2(2/\epsilon^2)}$ is the finite-size correction of the asymptotic equipartition property (Theorem 9 of Ref. [TCR09], with $\eta\le 2+\sqrt2$ for a binary variable and classical side information, valid for $S\ge\tfrac85\log_2(2/\epsilon^2)$), evaluated with smoothing parameter $\epsilon=\epsilon_{\rm sec}/4$, and the last two terms are the correctness and leftover-hash terms of Ref. [T25]. We keep the parameter-estimation and hashing budget of Ref. [T25] unchanged and charge the smoothing to an additional $\epsilon_{\rm sec}/4$, so the total secrecy parameter of the stated numbers is $5\epsilon_{\rm sec}/4$. A more conservative variant replaces $S^-_{\bar K}[1-I_E]-\Delta_{\rm AEP}$ by $-S^-_{\bar K}\log_2 p_{\rm guess}$ with $p_{\rm guess}$ the adversary's per-block guessing probability ($1-\gamma_b$ or $P^{\rm ad}_b$, mixed over the two block types), which needs no equipartition step. The use of the upper bound $\phi_Z^+$ in $I_E$ is conservative because $I_E$ increases with $D$ in both models.

If A2 fails, the adversary may hold her ancillas until the parities are public and the entropic-uncertainty term of Ref. [T25] must be restored; the formula then coincides with Eq. (93) of that work because every other term is shared. The key length vanishes for $\phi_Z^+\ge1/4$ by construction. Composability is claimed within the model: once the adversary's side information is classical, the leftover hash lemma against classical side information applies, and the security parameter is $\epsilon_{\rm sec}$ plus the probability that A1 fails during the run.

## 6. Implementation considerations

The AD stage adds $b-1$ exclusive-or operations and one comparison per block, fewer than one operation per raw bit, against several hundred operations per bit for belief-propagation decoding of an LDPC code; hardware key-distillation engines [Walenta14, Constantin17, Cheng25] therefore remain limited by the decoder after an AD stage is inserted, and this remains true whether or not the announcements are delayed. The timelock itself is a buffer of depth $\tau_{\rm ann}$ or an authenticated-encryption wrapper under a post-quantum key-encapsulation mechanism. Given Section 5, these are not costs worth paying for a $Z$-keyed protocol.

## 7. Discussion

The negative result rests on three steps whose scope should be stated. First, the attack family is the symmetric collective attack of Refs. [FGGNP97, GW99]; the reduction of coherent attacks to collective ones for a permutation-invariant protocol follows the post-selection technique of Christandl, König and Renner, which applies to any fixed adversary map and hence to the timelock-restricted class, at a polynomial cost in the security parameter; we have not carried out the decoy-state version of this argument. Second, joint measurements across blocks are not covered by the semidefinite programme; since the result is already a collapse of the gain, a cross-block advantage for the adversary could only strengthen it. Third, the semidefinite programme was solved for $b\le3$; the agreement with the adaptive value at every point suggests a structural reason (the pairs $\{\psi_a,\psi_{\bar a}\}$ are related by diagonal reflections that commute with the global flip, so a common optimal measurement exists), which we have not proven for general $b$.

Two observations about the reference formula arose in the course of the work. The bound on the logical phase-error rate of the distilled bits, Eq. (19) of Ref. [T25], is proven monotone in the single-photon error rates only on the domain $\phi_X+\phi_Z<1/2$ (Appendix B of that work), because blocks accepted with all $b$ bits flipped carry no phase error: at $\phi_X=0.3$ and $b=7$ the expression falls from 0.499 to 0.25 as $\phi_Z$ rises from 0.3 to 0.5. An unconstrained optimisation of the protocol parameters leaves the domain by driving the estimated single-photon $Z$ error toward one half through a vanishing test set, and then certifies key at QBERs above the asymptotic limit. Any implementation must enforce $\phi_X^++\phi_Z^+<1/2$ as an acceptance condition; we do so, and all four thresholds of Ref. [T25] lie inside the domain. Separately, the 25% figure of Ref. [GW99] is often quoted as the incoherent-attack limit for BB84 with two-way post-processing; Section 3 shows that it is the limit for incoherent *measurement* of the ancillas, and that a collective attack with a joint block measurement, which involves no quantum memory beyond the measurement, already reaches the 20% of Ref. [Chau02].

What, then, can a timelock do for a BB84-type protocol? Hiding the parities does not help because the parity-conditioned discrimination problems are compatible. Hiding the basis does not help BB84 at all, and helps six-state only without advantage distillation, where the memoryless-adversary threshold of Ref. [BAL11] applies; a timelock of delay $\tau_{\rm ann}>t_{\rm coh}$ is in fact the operational way to realise the memoryless assumption of that work. Once advantage distillation is used, the block gives the adversary a multi-copy discrimination problem in which the basis-conditioned optimal measurements become compatible again, and the unconditional two-way limits of 20% and 27.6% take over. MUB-QCT [VA20] escapes this because its $d+1$ bases in dimension $d$ do not admit a common measurement and because it does not rely on a parity-announcing post-processing step; the lesson of the present work is that for qubit protocols the basis is the only lever a timelock has, and advantage distillation removes it.

## 8. Conclusion

Keeping the parity and syndrome announcements of advantage-distillation post-processing hidden from an eavesdropper for longer than her quantum memory survives raises the finite-key QBER threshold of decoy-state BB84 only if she is restricted to measuring her ancillas one at a time (then 17.3% becomes 20.7–21.9% at $10^{14}$ pulses, with the 25% entanglement limit as asymptote). If she may measure the ancillas of a block jointly, even without adaptivity, she loses nothing by the delay: the asymptotic threshold is 20%, the finite-key threshold returns to 17.3–18.4%, and unconditional security already achieves essentially the same. Hiding the basis as well, which does help a memoryless adversary without advantage distillation for the six-state protocol, does not compound with advantage distillation: two-bit blocks with hidden, independently chosen bases already return the adversary to within $10^{-3}$ of the adaptive value, and the joint-measurement model reproduces the unconditional limits of 20% (BB84) and 27.6% (six-state) in closed form. The classical 25% limit of Gisin and Wolf is a per-ancilla statement, and the key-length formula of Treplin et al. must be used within its stated domain.
